America is building again — and it is short the materials to do it

Data centers, reshored factories, housing, the grid. The build-out is here, and the key structural materials it runs on are increasingly not made here:

Finished steel imported~1 ton in 5
Aluminum met by imports~85%
Primary smelters left4, from 33
Steel share of a data center~60%

Data center operators are already flagging structural-steel shortages, and aluminum is on the federal Critical Minerals List. Two of the core materials America builds with are significantly imported, and tariffs cut imports years before new domestic capacity can replace them. The gap lands on price and lead times.

More of the old mills won’t close the gap — not in time

Billions per plant

  • Aluminum: the Inola smelter is a ~$4B JV — needed a $500M federal grant to advance
  • Steel: Nucor’s Apple Grove sheet mill: $4.0B, 2021→2027 announce-to-ship

10+ year development timelines

  • Massive power needs — >1 GW continuous per smelter, competing with data centers
  • Complex logistics — Inola runs on Guinean bauxite, “vertically integrated” an ocean away
  • Cyclical economics — Century idled Hawesville over “soaring energy prices”

Community pushback

  • Pollution and power — Inola is permitted >1 t/day of hydrogen fluoride
  • Oklahoma’s AG has sued to block it — 3,400-signature petition; unanimous Muscogee Nation resolution

Closing America’s materials gap needs a different kind of mill: small enough to finance, fast enough to replicate, fed by something America already has everywhere.

    With the texture, colors, and natural warmth of wood.
