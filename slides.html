Closing America’s materials gap needs a different kind of mill: small enough to site easily, fast enough to scale to the need, using raw materials that continuously grow everywhere. It should be a mill people actually want in their hometown.

Appendix · Mass Timber

Augmenting mass timber: hybrid SUPERWOOD-glulam beams

Bending stiffness and strength are governed by a beam's outer fibers. Replacing just the outer laminations of a glulam beam with SUPERWOOD transforms its performance — while the section stays wood, workable, and beautiful.

Benefits over steel beams

  • Aesthetics — exposed structure with the warmth of wood
  • Ease of installation and lightness
  • Lower cost
  • Carbon impact — carbon-storing instead of carbon-intensive

Appendix · Data Centers

Data centers are booming — and they are built from steel

US data center construction spending has surged roughly 5× in two years, making data centers one of the largest drivers of commercial steel demand in the country.

~60% of a US data center is steel

Structural frame, joists and deck, rebar, equipment platforms, mezzanines, catwalks and racking — steel at nearly every level.

Operators are worried about steel supply

Data center companies are concerned about a shortage of structural steel right now — a direct opening for a scalable, domestic alternative.

The industry is already exploring wood

Meta and Microsoft have both demonstrated a two-thirds reduction in the carbon footprint of a data center building by going to mass timber (CLT) floors. With SUPERWOOD, we can reduce the building footprint much further — and most of the content of the building as well.

The longer-term opportunity

With roughly 60% of a data center being steel, the long-term prize is displacing most of it — full structural shells and foundations, plus much of the building's contents: racking, platforms, walkways and enclosures.

Appendix · Data Centers

SUPERWOOD data centers — fast, cost-effective, carbon-storing

01 · Faster, easier builds

Up to 10× the strength-to-weight ratio of steel — easier to transport and manipulate, faster to assemble and disassemble, for mission-critical infrastructure.

02 · Safer construction and operations

Steel is heavy, sharp, and conductive — a persistent injury risk. SUPERWOOD is 6× lighter, without sharp edges, and electrically non-conductive — with programmable conductive pathways as a future capability.

03 · Biophilic, community-friendly design

Natural beauty and texture that exceeds industrial-grade standards — a way to humanize hyperscale campuses facing public opposition, inside and out.

04 · Carbon storage

A bio-based material that stores biogenic carbon and emits far less in production than steel or concrete — a net carbon sink that cuts embodied carbon per functional unit.

Longer term — fully optimized building shells and foundations

Foundations

Lightweight, insulated foundations that can be installed more quickly than concrete, may require lower geotechnical requirements and timelines, and potentially be movable.

Building shells — optimized space frame structures

Lightweight, modular space frames allow quick-to-install integrated shell systems. Medium term, various wall and truss components can be replaced on an individual basis. Longer term, the entire shell can be optimized for weight, cost, and speed of assembly.
