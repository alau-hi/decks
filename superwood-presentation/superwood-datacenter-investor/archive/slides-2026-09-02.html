Why Data Centers

Why data centers?

01

Fast-growing market, hungry for materials

Construction is backlogged and operators tell us they face a shortage of structural steel. Supply chain and timeline are their first concern — and a domestic material that is lighter to ship and faster to erect answers it directly.

02

Large-volume offtake potential

Hyperscalers build repeatable campuses to a standard basis of design. One campus envelope program is on the order of SuperMill One's entire annual output; a basis-of-design win is SuperMill Two-scale demand.

03

Desire for low carbon and community acceptance

Microsoft and Meta already build data-center structures with mass timber (35% and ~41% embodied-carbon cuts on the elements replaced), reported through EC3 — co-created by our advisor Don Davies. Noise and visual mass drive moratoria; warm, quiet, biophilic exteriors change what the planning commission sees.

04

A wedge into structural — a market hungry for solutions

Replacing structural steel is the first thing new customers ask about. The mass-timber code pathway lets SUPERWOOD qualify as a new, far stronger species — and what is qualified for a data center is qualified for the broader structural market.

The Impact

Embodied carbon and circularity — labeled as the projections they are

Impact · The Vision

Can the world's data centers be a carbon sink?

Data centers are poised for massive growth. We asked: can we build them as long-term stores of carbon rather than massive emitters — and earn the social license to operate and scale? We think this is the way to do it:

POWERED by carbon-free electricity

Maximize efficiency and flexibility of load, then power with renewable energy, nuclear, biomass, and potentially BECCS — bio-energy with carbon capture and storage. Serve the grid.

CONSTRUCTED from waste biomaterials

Replace today's carbon-intensive materials with carbon-storing materials — designed for reuse at end of life to maximize lifecycle carbon benefit.

Steel is the lever, SUPERWOOD is the enabler. Steel sits at nearly every level of a data center — frame, joists and deck, platforms, racking, fences and screens. Replacing it element by element with a carbon-storing material is how the building's embodied ledger moves.

Data Center Applications

Fast, cost-effective, carbon-storing data centers

01

Faster, easier builds

Up to 10× the strength-to-weight ratio of steel — lighter to ship and lift, faster to assemble and disassemble, with a short domestic supply chain.

02

Safer construction and operations

Steel is heavy, sharp, and conductive. SUPERWOOD is one-sixth the weight, without sharp edges, and electrically non-conductive.

03

Biophilic, community-friendly design

Natural beauty and texture that meets industrial-grade demands — a way to humanize hyperscale campuses facing public opposition, inside and out.

04

Lower embodied carbon per element

A bio-based material that stores biogenic carbon and is projected to emit far less in production than steel — verified element by element as the LCA lands.
