## Part 1 — Where the deck is weak

### Bottom line

The deck is the customer deck with the Company section removed and a Customers section added. It
describes the material and the applications competently, and its claims discipline is better than the
July version (projections labeled, renders labeled, standards named). But it never makes the investor
argument. A reader finishes it knowing that SUPERWOOD could be used in data centers and that three
hyperscalers are talking to InventWood. They cannot size the opportunity in dollars, date it, see what
gates it, or see what could kill it. Twelve of 27 slides do not earn their place. The Impact section is
the weakest quarter and the one most likely to draw a hostile question.

### Argument gaps

1. **No investor "so what".** The cover says "companion to Super Mills America", but nothing connects
   data centers to the raise. What is one campus worth to InventWood at each horizon? What does a
   basis-of-design win do to SuperMill Two's offtake case? The model already exists in
   `analyses/materials-mass-and-replacement.md` (immediate skins 1.4–3.1M sf per GW; medium-term
   structural 16–65 kt per GW; plant-years of SuperMill One and Two) and none of it is on a slide in
   dollar terms. Derivation available today: 1.4–3.1M sf × $19/sf (interior realization, Super Mills
   register, internal) ≈ $27–59M per GW of campus in immediate skins alone; at $25/sf exterior (Jon,
   register context only, never printed) ≈ $35–78M. [derived from estimates, conf: L until Alex
   confirms the price basis]

2. **"Why not CLT?" is never answered.** Slide 4 and the Microsoft/Meta cards use hyperscaler CLT
   projects as validation. CLT is code-approved (PRG 320, IBC Type IV), cheaper, and already inside
   those customers' programs. The deck must say what SUPERWOOD does that CLT cannot: replace steel
   sections rather than concrete slabs, exterior durability, thin high-strength members, fences and
   screens. A sharp investor asks this on slide 4; the deck has no slide for it. Same for the other
   incumbents on the low-carbon shopping list: EAF and green steel, metal and fiber-cement rain
   screens, composite fencing.

3. **Engagement is presented as traction.** Four slides (22–25), zero dates, zero dollars, zero
   installed material, zero signed documents. "Broad engagement", "engaged for over a year",
   "engagement just begun" all read as "conversations". Missing: a stage ladder (conversation →
   samples → mockup → paid pilot → PO → basis of design), where each account sits, the next
   milestone and its date, and what each would buy first and in what quantity. Google gets a full
   slide for two bullets.

4. **The structural thesis depends on SuperMill Two, and the deck never says so plainly.** Today's
   product envelope (boards to 8" × 16' × 3/8") cannot make a truss, a roof deck or an enclosure.
   Structural needs SuperMill Two veneers and sections, and SuperMill Two is unsited and is what the
   raise funds. Slide 20 reads as if trusses are near. The investor question — what can you actually
   sell a data center in 2026–2028 — has a short honest answer (skins, screens, fences, interiors from
   ~1M sf/yr shared with every other market) that the deck avoids.

5. **Certification is unpriced.** Slide 10 is honest that nothing has started, but its title says "the
   code pathway is mapped". No owner, lab, cost, duration or budget. For an investor this is the
   schedule risk and it is invisible. The claim "trusses carry no fire-resistance requirement"
   (slides 10, 20, 25) is too simple: in IBC Type I/II noncombustible construction, wood in roof
   construction is permitted only as fire-retardant-treated wood under IBC 603.1 conditions, or the
   building goes to a heavy-timber/Type IV route. The gate is combustibility and an FRTW-type listing,
   not the hourly rating. [conf: M — confirm with a code consultant before it prints again]

6. **No risk slide.** FM Global and insurer acceptance of a combustible material in the white space,
   single-plant capacity allocation, price versus incumbents, competing low-carbon materials,
   hyperscaler 18–24-month design cycles, LCA outcome. An investor deck without these reads as naive.

7. **The Impact section is marketing, not evidence.** Slide 12's POWERED column (nuclear, BECCS,
   "serve the grid") is not InventWood's business. Slide 14 is a collage under "we create an economic
   engine powering a healthier planet". Slide 15 (12.5 Mt/yr displaces 50% of US steel) is a company-
   level claim that belongs in Super Mills America. Only slide 13 carries numbers, and they are the
   Canva set (0.5 / 1.3 kg per kg) while the Jan 2026 TEM says 0.39 / 1.8 per t — a conflict the
   outline flagged and nobody resolved.

8. **Speed is the data center benefit and it is a bullet.** Time-to-power is the metric hyperscalers
   optimize. The original deck's "Enabling speed and scale of deployment" slide (prefabricated
   components, design for assembly and disassembly, reusable and reconfigurable, plug-and-play) is the
   most data-center-specific argument in the whole family and it was dropped. The companion keeps
   "faster to assemble and disassemble" as half a bullet on slide 17.

### Open items, checked against this deck

- **60%-steel stat**: not printed here. Good. Still printed on Super Mills America's Gap slide and
  appendix. The model's ex-concrete derivation lands near 70% steel on its own assumptions, which is
  consistent in order of magnitude but is a derivation, not a source.
- **"90% emissions reduction"**: not printed here. Derivation per the outline: 72–78% per kg vs
  average steel; ~90% only per functional unit at ~0.5 substitution factor; far less vs EAF. Keep it off.
- **Pre-LCA labeling**: present on slide 13 in body copy. Hu / Notre Dame named. OK.
- **Hu H-index 198**: founder does not appear in this deck. Moot here.
- **Price basis ×1.7**: this deck prints no prices at all. Super Mills America's Cost Roadmap prints
  price/cost ratios of 1.55 (SuperMill One: $22,425 / $14,472 per m³), 2.53 (SuperMill Two: $8,222 /
  $3,244) and 2.4–2.5 (ChipMill). None is 1.7. Before any dollar figure prints here, Alex to say which
  basis applies to data center skins (the $19/sf interior realization and $25/sf exterior in the SMA
  register are the only realized prices on file).

## Part 2 — Revised storyline

**Thesis:** Data centers are the customer that turns SUPERWOOD from a premium skin into a structural
material, and the demand anchor for SuperMill Two.

**Arc (each title is a claim):**

1. The buyer: data centers are the fastest-growing buyer of structural steel, short of it, and
   already building with wood.
2. The gap they still have: wood that replaces steel does not exist yet. Why not CLT.
3. The size: what a campus is made of, what SUPERWOOD can address by horizon, and what that is worth
   to InventWood in dollars and plant-years.
4. The fit: strength, properties, speed and modularity — the data-center-specific benefits, each with
   its standard or an honest gap.
5. The path: applications staged as revenue stages; certification as a priced, owned program;
   structural starting now from today's boards and scaling with SuperMill Two; the long-term vision
   of prefabricated envelopes and foundations, labeled as vision.
6. The evidence: every named account on one engagement ladder with a next milestone.
7. The carbon claim, sized and labeled, with both steel baselines.
8. Risks and what has to be true; the undated path to first projects; close.

What the deck does not do: company, team, mills, cost roadmap, raise. Cover and slide 2 point to
Super Mills America.
